Abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) still struggle to reason about space in video, and their benchmark scores do not reveal whether they fail at perception or at reasoning. We introduce SPLIT (Separating Perception from LLM Inference with Tools), a training-free harness that builds a metric 3-D reconstruction of a video once and gives a base VLM tools to query it. On eight spatial benchmarks of static scenes, dynamic videos, and multi-image sets, SPLIT raises the base VLM’s accuracy by up to 27%, and the gains extend to open-weight base VLMs. Crucially, SPLIT is also faster than the base VLM alone: it answers up to 1.4× faster and generates up to 3.8× fewer tokens, at lower cost. SPLIT helps most when the objects in a question are never co-visible, where 3-D geometry is most useful. Unlike an end-to-end VLM, SPLIT enables us to differentiate between perceptual and reasoning failures in VLMs. We replace its tool measurements with ground-truth measurements and find that SPLIT makes 3× fewer errors, reaching over 90% accuracy, and that smaller open-weight VLMs can come within 2% of Gemini 3.1 Pro. We also find that localizing objects and recovering their 3-D geometry improve different tasks. Perception, not reasoning, limits current VLMs.

Method

How SPLIT answers a question

Answering a spatial question about a video parallels the distinction in human cognition between direct, intuitive (“System 1”) responses and deliberate, step-by-step (“System 2”) reasoning. A base VLM answering directly from video frames resembles System 1. SPLIT resembles System 2: it plans explicit steps that measure the scene with perception tools before answering.
